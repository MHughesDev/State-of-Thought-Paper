Persistent multi-agent cognition requires a single object in which what is known, what is provisional, and what may persist are simultaneously representable and auditable. We develop State of Thought: a layered directed typed multigraph whose vertices are thoughts, whose edges carry a finite type vocabulary (support, contradiction, provenance, and contract-fixed types), and whose labels distinguish abstraction layer, open versus closed status, persistence scope, and optional epistemic stance. The formalism unifies three regions—globally closed structure, query-local session structure, and an external interface into the evidence layer—and a single closure map that promotes session-local candidates to global persistence under explicit policies. Admissibility is path-based along designated downward types to layer-0 witnesses that are themselves globally persistent. Conflict handling and parallel-agent reconciliation are policy objects, composed with an admissibility gate and provenance subpolicy inside a decomposed closure operator. We give a reference policy triple and a symbolic worked trace. The main text records definitional consequences, one conjecture on admissibility stability under closure, and open questions. We do not claim implementation results, empirical behavior, or general graph-theoretic theorems beyond what is stated. Related work situates the contribution relative to classical shared-memory cognitive architectures, recent tool-augmented and memory-centric language-model agents, and formal multilayer network models.
